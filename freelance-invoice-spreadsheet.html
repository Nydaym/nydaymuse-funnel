Freelance invoice tracker & aging spreadsheet

Columns that matter · aging buckets · cash clarity · updated 2026

A freelance invoice spreadsheet is not a tax filing. It is a living list of what you billed, what is open, and what is late — so you can answer “what hits my account this week?” without opening seventeen email threads.

You can build one from scratch in any sheet app. Or you can use a ready workbook like Ops ($12) that also holds client context. Either way, the structure below is what stops aging from becoming a surprise.

Minimum columns (do not skip these)

ColumnWhy it exists
Invoice IDUnique reference for email and bank notes.
ClientWho owes you — filter and sort by relationship.
Issue dateWhen you sent it; baseline for aging.
Due dateContractual deadline — aging starts here.
Amount / currencyExact number; avoid mixing currencies in one total.
StatusDraft / Sent / Partial / Paid / Written off.
Paid dateCloses the loop; feeds cash timing.
Days overdueFormula: today − due date (if unpaid).

Optional but useful: project name, payment method, PO number, late-fee flag, and a “last chased” date so you know when you last sent a reminder.

Aging buckets freelancers actually use

Accounting packages use 30/60/90. Solo freelancers often need tighter buckets because cash runs out faster than corporate AP cycles:

  • Current — not yet due
  • 1–7 days — first reminder window
  • 8–14 days — second nudge
  • 15–30 days — final notice / pause work
  • 30+ — escalate or write-off decision

Sum open amounts by bucket on a dashboard row. That single view is cash clarity: not “I think they will pay,” but “$X is 15+ days late.”

Habit: Update status the same day money lands. A tracker that is three days stale is how late invoices hide.

Cash view vs invoice list

An invoice list answers “what is unpaid?” A cash view answers “what is likely to arrive, and when?” You get there by combining:

  1. Open invoices sorted by due date
  2. Expected pay week (or “unknown”)
  3. Known client behavior (always 10 days late vs usually on time)

Client behavior belongs next to the money — not in a separate notebook you never open. That is why Ops pairs a money sheet with a client sheet instead of leaving notes buried in chat.

DIY checklist (one evening)

  1. Create one “Invoices” tab with the columns above.
  2. Add a “Status” dropdown so filters stay clean.
  3. Add Days overdue as a formula, not a manual number.
  4. Add a summary block: total open, total 15+, total paid this month.
  5. Create a “Clients” tab: rate, terms (net-15/30), preferred payment link, notes.
  6. Every Friday: filter unpaid, send reminders, update “last chased.”
