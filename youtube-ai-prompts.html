Where AI helps (and where it does not)

AI is useful after you pick a topic and know one proof story — not instead of watching your own analytics. Good prompts ask the model to use only your inputs, mark unknowns in [BRACKETS], avoid fake subscriber or revenue claims, and return spoken-aloud lines you can edit. Weak prompts say “make it viral” and invent urgency you cannot back up on camera.

Hooks and titles without clickbait lies

Hook prompts work best when you constrain length, pattern mix (curiosity, mistake, result, list), and “must avoid” lines — especially fake view counts. Title and thumbnail workshops should return options with a one-line why, so you choose packaging that matches the video you actually filmed.

Say the hook out loud. If you would not open a video that way on camera, revise before you record.

Scripts that stay filmable

Long outlines should map cold hook, promise, body beats, proof, mistakes, and CTA — spoken language, not essay paragraphs. Shorts scripts need a tighter arc: hook, one point, proof or demo, CTA. Aim’s SAMPLE I/O shows the shape; you still time it on camera and cut what does not land.
