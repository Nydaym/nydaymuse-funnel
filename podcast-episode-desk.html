Episode workflow that actually ships

  1. Idea — title and guest hypothesis before you pitch.
  2. Booked — confirm date; send the guest one-pager so they know format and tech.
  3. Recorded → Edited — move status when audio is in; keep publish date visible.
  4. Live — run the publish checklist: audio, shownotes, chapters, social clips.

Status values (Idea / Booked / Recorded / Edited / Live) keep the calendar honest. If everything stays “Idea,” you do not have a show — you have a backlog.

Rule of thumb: Write show-notes chapters the same day you edit. Waiting until publish day is how “coming soon” timestamps stay wrong for months.

Guest outreach without awkward silence

A good pitch names why this guest fits this episode, offers a clear time window, and asks one yes/no question. Confirm messages restate date, length, and tech (Zoom, Riverside, local recorder). Thank-you notes should arrive after the record — and the clip-ask should make sharing easy with a suggested caption. Pod’s swipe file is copy-paste scaffolding; you still sound like yourself.

Show notes that listeners can scan

Open with a one-sentence hook. Add a short bio. List chapters with timestamps when you have them. Put links in one block so show notes are not a scavenger hunt. The live preview in Pod helps you catch empty sections before you paste into your host dashboard.

Batch recording without losing the publish trail

Many hosts record two or three episodes in one afternoon. That only works if Record date and Publish date stay separate. Mark Recorded when the file lands, keep Publish date on the future slot, and do not mark Live until the checklist is done. Duration helps you spot episodes that ran long before you promise a tight weekly cadence to listeners.

When a guest cancels, leave the row — change status back to Idea or delete after you note the reason in your own calendar. The desk will not replace your host analytics; it replaces the sticky notes that usually hold guest emails and half-written shownotes.
