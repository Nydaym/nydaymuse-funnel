A simple shoot workflow

  1. Inquiry — reply with timeline questions and a clear next step.
  2. Booked — lock date, location, and balance owed in one row.
  3. Shot day — print the call sheet; check gear and family groupings.
  4. Edited → Delivered — track gallery URL, expiry, and revision count until the delivery is closed.

Status values in Shot (Inquiry / Booked / Shot / Edited / Delivered) match how most solo shooters already think — without forcing a sales pipeline metaphor.

Rule of thumb: If the gallery has no expiry logged, set one the day you send the link. Quiet clients often mean forgotten bookmarks — not unhappy buyers.

Call sheets that fit a solo kit

Big production call sheets are overkill for a two-person wedding or a brand half-day. Shot’s print view keeps timeline, location, and gear on one page you can fold into a pocket. Fill it the night before while the shot list checkboxes are still fresh, then stop rearranging folders on your phone in the parking lot.

Client emails without reinventing tone

Inquiry replies should set expectations: packages, deposit, and what you need from them. Gallery-ready notes should tell them how long the link lasts and how revisions work. Closing revisions needs a firm, kind line so “one more tweak” does not become unpaid editing forever. The swipe file is a starting point — you still personalize names and dates.

Wedding vs portrait vs brand — same desk, different fields

Type the shoot as Wedding, Portrait, Brand, or Event so filters stay useful when your calendar mixes Saturday ceremonies with Tuesday headshots. Balance owed belongs on the shoot row — not only in your banking app — so you see who still owes a second payment before you unlock the full gallery. Location and date keep the call sheet honest when you second-shoot or travel.

If you outgrow SAMPLE rows, export JSON before a browser clear, or import the Notion CSVs into a database you already use. The desk stays the day-of tool; your archive can live elsewhere.
