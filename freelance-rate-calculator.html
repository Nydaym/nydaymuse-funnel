How do you calculate a freelance project price?

Multiply your hourly rate by estimated project hours, then add any clearly defined rush allowance, contingency, and materials. State which amount each percentage applies to. Use the result alongside a written scope, revision limit, and delivery timeline so the client can see what the price includes.

Simple formula (before tiers)

This illustrative method calculates both percentage allowances from the labor base; it does not compound them.

base = hourly × hours rush = base × (rush% / 100) contingency = base × (contingency% / 100) total = base + rush + contingency + materials

That total is your honest single-option quote. Tiers come next when the client wants choice without a free custom redesign for every budget.

Worked example: a 20-hour project

For a hypothetical project at $75 per hour, 20 estimated hours gives a $1,500 labor base. A 10% rush allowance adds $150, a 15% contingency adds $225, and $80 of materials brings the total to $1,955. Both percentages use the $1,500 base. These are example inputs, not suggested rates for your work.

Before sending that amount, name the deliverables and revision rounds it covers. If the client removes a deliverable, revise the hours and allowances before calculating the smaller package.

Core / Recommended / Comprehensive

Three packages let buyers self-select. Keep the differences real — scope and outcomes, not just “more hours” with the same deliverable.

  • Core — minimum viable outcome; tight scope; fewer revisions.
  • Recommended — the option you want most clients to pick; best balance.
  • Comprehensive — extras, research, extra rounds, or faster turnaround.

How to use numbers in a proposal

  1. Run the calculator with conservative hours.
  2. Lock a floor you will not go below.
  3. Offer three tiers with clear “included / not included.”
  4. Paste totals into the proposal investment section.
  5. If they ask for cheaper, shrink scope — do not silently cut the floor.

Freelance rate calculator questions

Does a rate calculator choose my hourly rate?

No. A project calculator uses the rate and hours you enter. Estimate your delivery, meetings, and revision time first, then check whether the resulting project price fits the scope you can commit to.

Should rush and contingency percentages compound?

There is no single method assumed by this guide. In the worked example, both apply to the labor base separately. Whatever method you use, label the calculation basis and check the total before sharing the quote.

What is the difference between a quote and a proposal?

A quote sets out a price for defined work. A proposal also explains the client's goal, deliverables, exclusions, timeline, and next step. Quote includes separate offline HTML files for the rate calculator and printable proposal lite.
