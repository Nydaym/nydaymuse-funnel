Why freelancers skip onboarding (and pay later)

  • Welcome email feels “optional” when everyone is excited to start.
  • Assets arrive late — or as low-res screenshots in chat.
  • Feedback channels multiply (email + Slack + WhatsApp + comments).
  • Decision owners stay vague until a late-stage revision war.

A lightweight Notion hub does not replace your contract. It makes the first week predictable so delivery can start on time.

Welcome email (swipe)

Subject: Welcome — [Project] kickoff Hi [Name], Excited to start [Project]. Here is how we begin: 1) Kickoff checklist (below / Notion link) 2) Assets & access list — please send by [DATE] 3) Comms: we use [EMAIL / SLACK], responses within [N] business days Kickoff call: [DATE / TIME / LINK] Deposit invoice: [LINK] (if not already paid) Reply with any blockers. Looking forward to a clean start. [Your name]

Freelance kickoff checklist

  1. Contract signed + deposit received (or terms confirmed in writing).
  2. Primary contact and decision owner named.
  3. Goals / success criteria in one short paragraph.
  4. Assets list sent; due date set.
  5. Access: tools, accounts, folders, brand kit.
  6. Comms channel + meeting cadence agreed.
  7. First deliverable date on the calendar.
  8. Invoice schedule and payment method confirmed.

Assets request (keep it specific)

Vague “send everything” emails fail. Ask for formats and owners:

  • Logo: SVG / PNG, light and dark if available
  • Brand guide or color/font notes
  • Existing copy, screenshots, or competitor links
  • Analytics / CMS / hosting access (role-based, not shared passwords in chat)
  • Stakeholder list with who must approve

Comms expectations (short is better)

Put this in Notion and paste a summary in the welcome email:

  • Primary channel: one place for decisions (not five).
  • Response window: e.g. 1 business day for blockers.
  • Feedback rounds: how many are in scope.
  • Urgent path: what counts as urgent and who to ping.
